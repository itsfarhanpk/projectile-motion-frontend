Projectile Motion Calculator

Projectile motion is the curved path an object follows after it is launched, when gravity is the only force acting on it. The motion splits into two independent parts: a steady horizontal drift and a vertical rise-and-fall. Enter a launch speed and angle to get the range, maximum height, and flight time, then rotate the 3D view to see why the path is a parabola.

  • g = 9.81 m/s²
  • No air resistance
  • Launch and landing at the same height

How to read the 3D view

The scene is drawn from the same numbers as the results, so every arrow and marker has a formula behind it.

The cyan arrow never changes

The horizontal part of the launch speed is v cos θ. Nothing pushes the object sideways, so this stays constant for the whole flight. That is why the range is simply this speed multiplied by the flight time.

The violet arrow shrinks, flips, and grows

The vertical part starts at v sin θ. Gravity removes 9.81 m/s from it every second, so it reaches zero at the top and then grows downward. The violet marker shows the maximum height.

Gravity points straight down

The red arrow is the only force in this model. Because it acts only downward, the steady horizontal drift combined with the changing vertical speed traces a parabola.

The ground line is the range

The line along the floor is the landing distance. Rotating the camera makes it clear that the object stays in one vertical plane: this ideal model has no sideways motion at all.

Equations used

  • Range (v² × sin 2θ) / g
  • Maximum height (v² × sin² θ) / (2g)
  • Flight time (2 × v × sin θ) / g
  • Position at time t x = v cos θ · t   y = v sin θ · t − ½ g t²

θ is the launch angle measured from the horizontal. The last pair of equations produces the points for both the 3D path and the 2D graph.
